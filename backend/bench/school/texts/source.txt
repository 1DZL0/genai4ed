The July Crisis, 1914

On 28 June 1914, Archduke Franz Ferdinand, the heir to the throne of Austria-Hungary, was shot dead with his wife Sophie in Sarajevo, the capital of Bosnia. The gunman, Gavrilo Princip, was a young Bosnian Serb linked to the Black Hand, a secret Serbian nationalist group. Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia. Once Germany had promised its full support, Austria-Hungary sent Serbia a list of harsh demands on 23 July. Serbia accepted most of them, but not all, and on 28 July Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia. Russia, which saw itself as Serbia's protector, began to mobilise its huge army. Germany declared war on Russia on 1 August and on Russia's ally France two days later. Germany's plan was to defeat France quickly by marching through Belgium. When German troops entered Belgium, whose neutrality Britain had promised to protect, Britain declared war on Germany on 4 August. In just over five weeks, one murder had drawn most of Europe's great powers into war.
